# From a design problem to useful reference evidence

Use only a lens relevant to the open decision. These are our research prompts,
informed by the linked source cards; they are not claims that the books prescribe
these queries or that the reference treatment will work here. Each source card
contains printed/PDF provenance and applicability limits.

| Open problem | Inspect in references | Improve or challenge | Verify beyond captures | Source rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Comparing alternatives requires remembering values | Which labels, units, costs, qualifications, and differences are visible together? What moves into detail? | Keep useful alignment; correct hidden totals, unhelpful repetition, or decoration that displaces differences. Compare disclosure with concurrent comparison. | Answer the actual choice question using real long content, narrow widths, and large text; observe understanding when it matters. | [JJ-03/07/13](sources/mind-in-mind.md), [DI-09/10/19](sources/designing-interfaces.md) |

## Touch-specific reference questions

Use [touch](touch.md) when the open question concerns target separation, an apparent
row affordance, gesture discovery, obscured feedback, or keyboard/chrome crowding.
Compare equivalent task states and challenge attractive but error-prone controls.
Treat hit geometry, grip comfort, and accidental activation as unknown from stills;
carry them into native or mobile-web checks. Source rationale: [Touch Design](sources/touch-design.md).

## A worked transfer, not a recipe

Suppose people choose items from several categories. A cart reference may expose
selected items and offer review; a bulk-management screen may show per-item marks
and a total. Neither proves a shared draft across categories. Retain those useful
feedback mechanisms, omit checkout/account machinery unless needed, and propose
an explicit draft/commit contract fitted to this product. A compact tray might
work at ordinary text sizes but need a dedicated review surface with large text.
Compare that with immediate reversible addition before deciding: the latter may
remove a review step but change the meaning of Cancel and increase correction work.

The causal hypothesis is that visible selection scope and review reduce recall
and uncertainty. A runtime check can verify counts, state continuity, and removal;
it cannot prove users find the treatment easier. Inspect the realized result,
state the remaining usability hypothesis, and revise when evidence contradicts it.

The quality standard comes from the person's goal, truthful behavior, deliberate
craft, platform fit, and evidence—not the prestige of the reference. Use
[inspection guidance](sources/inspection-methods.md), especially WI-03/07/08/10,
to challenge both precedents and this skill's recommendations fairly.
