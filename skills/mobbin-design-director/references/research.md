# Research that resolves design decisions

Mobbin supplies examples to examine, not a quality ceiling or user research.
Famous products can have weak interactions and visual execution. Retain what
serves this task, improve weaknesses, and reject unsuitable treatments.

Discover the live tools and schemas before calling them. Use screen search for
composition/states, flow search for journeys, and website-section search for
marketing sections when available. Match the actual platform. Keep task intent
and output destination consistent with the deliverable; send a concise generic
intent, not private project context. Use the connected service, not a scraper.

## Form a question the evidence can answer

Name the decision and the burden it may change: what must the person remember,
compare, infer, notice, enter, or recover? For consequential choices, articulate
a working explanation and a plausible rival. Translate **goal and context →
visible information/action/state → neutral query**. Investigate how total costs
are presented before commitment, rather than starting with a beautiful pricing
grid. Screens can show which costs appear together; improved comprehension is
a hypothesis requiring different evidence. Search named patterns after identifying
plausible mechanisms. Use the [research lenses](research-lenses.md) if useful.

Study owner references first. Direct products, adjacent tasks, and other categories
are search options, not required rings. Transfer behavior by matching goals,
objects, prerequisites, consequences, and use conditions. A cart may inform batch
review but not a financial operation's commitment semantics. Visual references
may cross task boundaries to explore typography, rhythm, imagery, or material;
visual fit does not establish behavioral fit. Seek alternatives that differ on
the open decision, rather than collecting near-identical fashionable screens.

## Retrieve, inspect, and refine

Use small batches. Inspect images before describing them; metadata is not visual
evidence. Record app, task/state, observed choice, stable Mobbin link, relevance,
and unknowns. Check the actual object and action: “card” may mean a payment card,
pass, or content tile. On mismatch, change one meaningful query term or investigate
an adjacent task for a named missing behavior. Deep search is not a relevance
certificate. Page on-target results; do not repeatedly page a mismatched query.
Use named-app queries or exclusions only when supported and useful.

Flow previews may be evenly sampled rather than complete. Compare preview
positions with the returned screen list. Inspect decision-relevant omitted images
using returned URLs and permitted temporary storage. Record positions inspected
and gaps; do not call three previews an inspected eight-screen flow. Use higher
resolution when small text or detail matters. If unreadable or unavailable, narrow
the claim. A full captured list still cannot reveal uncaptured branches.

Keep stable Mobbin links. Follow the tool's current image-use and usage-notice
requirements. Temporary image URLs expire; temporary reference images stay out
of Git. Cite a source for every app-specific observation, including a rejected
example when its weakness informs the recommendation.

## Compare equivalent moments and critique the reference

Align subgoal, object, available information, prior choices, and commitment state,
not screen index or title. One product may combine moments another separates.
Account for saved data, expertise, authorization, frequency, and other relevant
prerequisites. A saved draft, submitted request, and completed external result are
different states even when all show a checkmark.

| Moment | Person supplies / decides | Visible response | Context carried | Exit / recovery captured | Unknown |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Comparable task moment | Input, attention, permission | Information or state | Only what evidence supports | Actual captured branch | Unseen behavior |

Preserve captured action/connection information when supplied; adjacent images
alone do not establish the gesture between them. A missing error capture is
unknown, not proof of missing recovery. Mark handoffs to another app, person, or
service and any unseen work needed to finish the goal. Timing, persistence,
accessibility, and performance require more than screenshots.

For a useful reference, distinguish **retain / improve / reject** in a few lines:

- **Retain:** a mechanism or visual relationship that serves the task, and why.
- **Improve:** an observed weakness or predicted cost, with a proposed adaptation.
- **Reject:** a mismatched prerequisite, unnecessary step, misleading treatment,
  or visual choice that conflicts with this product's direction.

These are reasoning prompts, not mandatory filled columns for every result.
When a familiar pattern moves between platforms or jobs, recheck its original
problem and prerequisites. Common practice can become an anti-pattern in a new
context; repetition does not repair that mismatch. [ID-19](sources/interaction-design.md),
pp. 505–510, supplies this principle; the retain/improve/reject procedure
is our operational adaptation.
Separate observable defects, reasoned usability risks, and aesthetic preferences.
An unappealing screen can teach a useful mechanism; an attractive screen can
obscure consequences. Do not infer bad usability from taste, or successful use
from polish, popularity, or a familiar brand. Negative examples can inform a
stronger design; there is no need to adopt a whole reference or find an exemplary
app to authorize an original solution.

## Resolve the decision and remaining unknowns

Compare the strongest viable alternatives under equivalent task/content/state
conditions. Explain their prerequisites and costs. Repeated appearances show a
convention, not conversion, retention, or measured success. Use
[synthesis](synthesis.md) to preserve the mechanism through implementation.

Route consequential unknowns to evidence capable of answering them: another
capture for visible error treatment; source/runtime for persistence and commitment;
a native prototype for gestures or system behavior; intended users for actual
understanding and discoverability. Carry the remaining assumptions into the build
checks. More screenshots cannot settle an invisible behavior or user outcome.

Search fresh references for an open decision when available. Reuse settled
project decisions; do not re-search because a new turn began. Revisit evidence
when the question, platform, or assumptions change. Retrieval date is not capture
date or proof of current design.

Stop when additional evidence no longer changes viable mechanisms, tradeoffs,
or the recommendation for this decision. Before treating repetition as useful
stability, consider whether one implementation family or missing moment narrowed
the evidence. Broaden only if it could change the choice. Distinguish **decision
stability**, **insufficient coverage**, **budget stop**, and **tool failure**;
none proves the design space or catalog exhausted. There is no reference quota.

## Unavailable or insufficient Mobbin

State the specific limit once, then continue with project evidence, book
principles, current platform guidance, owner references, and labeled hypotheses.
Do not invent app observations or links, silently substitute unrelated images,
or turn missing tools into a universal design block. If the request requires
Mobbin-only research, report that narrower deliverable as incomplete.

Keep consequential evidence and decisions in the
[design packet](../templates/design-packet.md), not a separate document per step.
This research method is our synthesis; the books did not prescribe a Mobbin protocol.
