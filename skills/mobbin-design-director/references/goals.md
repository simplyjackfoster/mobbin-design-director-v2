# Goals, context, and evidence

Use for a meaningful new screen, flow, or uncertain redesign. Compress to a few
lines when the project already settles these questions.

## Establish the design problem

- **Person and situation:** relevant knowledge, frequency, environment, access
  needs, interruptions, and what they already know. Prefer behavioral distinctions
  over invented demographic biographies.
- **End goal:** the condition the person wants to reach. A requested form or
  dashboard is a possible solution, not itself the goal.
- **Experience goal:** how the interaction should feel in this situation: in
  control, reassured, capable, curious, energized. Make this influence concrete
  choices, not a list of adjectives.
- **Current path:** what works today, workarounds, observed friction, and important
  behavior to preserve. Inspect the current product before diagnosing it.
- **Constraints:** data availability, privacy/commitment, business requirements,
  project decisions, platform/target, content, time and implementation scope.
- **Success:** a checkable task outcome plus relevant comprehension, recovery,
  accessibility, or perceptual criteria. Do not invent metric baselines.

Separate requirements (compare total costs) from premature solutions (three
pricing cards). Ask the owner about product intent only when missing information
changes the solution materially; otherwise state a working assumption and proceed.

## Keep evidence kinds visible

| Kind | Supports | Does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Observed user behavior or research | What specified participants did in a stated context | Universal preference or performance |
| Product/runtime evidence | Current behavior, data, failures, constraints | Why users behave that way |
| Owner decision | Product direction and approved preference | Usability for every audience |
| Book principle | A reasoned design expectation under stated conditions | A measured result for this product |
| Platform/standard | Applicable convention or explicit requirement | That every custom alternative is forbidden |
| Mobbin observation | A visible implementation and captured sequence | Conversion, usability, hidden branches, capture freshness |
| Hypothesis | A testable explanation or proposed choice | A fact merely because agents agree |

Use one-line evidence labels in the packet where they matter. Avoid formal
evidence paperwork for every control.

Multiple sources do not automatically corroborate a conclusion: a screenshot,
an interview and an event log may concern different people, states or questions.
Keep the observation separate from its interpretation and proposed change; check
whether apparently conflicting evidence is actually comparable. This applies
[ID-10](sources/interaction-design.md), pp. 279–280 and 326–333.

Include permanent, temporary and situational access needs in the task context.
An agent imagining an impairment, or a designer briefly simulating one, does not
establish lived experience or the strategies people actually use. Treat such
exercises as hypotheses and involve relevant users when research is authorized
([ID-09](sources/interaction-design.md), ch. 1–2 and p. 457).

## When there is no user research

Build a provisional behavioral audience and realistic scenario from known product
facts. Name what is inferred. Favor reversible choices; do a task walkthrough;
identify the highest-value question for later observation. Do not block all design
work, fabricate interviews, or claim that competitor popularity validates a need.

If participant testing is requested, specify the audience, neutral task, starting
state, observations, and decision the study should inform. Recruiting or contacting
people is a separate action requiring the user's authorization.

## Evidence conflicts

Current user instructions define scope. Preserve approved product decisions while
surfacing concrete problems. Verify a suspected platform/accessibility violation
against current applicable guidance; try to preserve the intended direction while
fixing the issue. Separate technical migration cost from the UX argument. Context
determines which book principle applies; the count of supporting books is not a vote.
